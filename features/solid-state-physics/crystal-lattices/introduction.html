Crystal lattices

Any lattice point in such a crystal is located with respect to an origin by a vector \( \vec{R} \) of the form \begin{equation} \vec{R} = h\vec{a} + k\vec{b} + l\vec{c} \end{equation} Or in matrix form \begin{equation} \vec{R} = \left( \begin{array}{l} R_x \\ R_y \\ R_z \end{array} \right) = \left( \begin{array}{lll} a_x & b_x & c_x \\ a_y & b_y & c_y \\ a_z & b_z & c_z \end{array} \right) \left( \begin{array}{l} h \\ k \\ l \end{array} \right) \end{equation} where \( h \), \( k \) and \( l \) are integers. Each element of the matrix must be considered as projections of the vectors \( \vec{a} \), \( \vec{b} \) and \( \vec{c} \) onto the coordinates axes \( x \), \( y \) and \( z \) respectively.

The magnitude of the vector, considering a oblique space, is determined by \begin{equation} |\vec{R}|^2 = R^2 = h^2a^2 + k^2b^2 + l^2c^2 + 2hkab \cos \gamma + 2klbc \cos \alpha + 2hlac \cos \beta \end{equation}
